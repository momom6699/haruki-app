A running journal

Write down
the day you ran.

The road you took, the songs you heard, your photos.
The day is left on a single page.

Pre-register

Runners see
a different city.

Not the name of the intersection — the riverside, the shortcut
through the park, the road with no traffic lights.

Run.

One tap to start. Then put the phone away.

It stays.

Distance, route, the songs you heard, your photos. Once the run ends.

Read it again.

That day's diary, and a calendar that fills up.

Running is
not a score.

No rankings, no challenges. Fast days and slow days
stay as the same kind of day.

The same city,
again and again.

The roads you ran slowly become your own map. The ones you use most grow darker where they overlap.

No rankings, no challenges, no ads.

What you ran stays on your device.

  • Photos, music and weather kept with the record
